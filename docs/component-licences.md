# Component licences and distribution boundaries

Prepared 21 September 2026 from inspected source notices and recorded public-source checks. This is an evidence inventory for internal review, not a legal determination, new licence grant or authorization to publish. Public access, copyright permission, privacy/consent and authority to relicense are separate questions. No source owner was contacted.

This manifest updates the earlier [M4 rights audit](../readiness/m4-rights-audit.md): study-specific Workbench licence metadata is now verified, the ST002081 scientific count concern is reconciled, and Human-GEM version-specific citation is corrected. Those items are not unresolved author blockers. Exact historical CCLE terms, new distribution of participant-linked material and author-owned declarations remain open.

## Resolved technical issues versus author decisions

ST002081's retained 1,539 samples and 112 participant codes agree with the final paper. The supplement independently supports 112 codes, while containing more sample records. The repository narrative's unexplained 1,546/109 is retained as a provenance note; resolving its history is not required to use the final paper's supported count. See the [reconciliation](../readiness/m3-cohort-reconciliation.md). Human-GEM v2.0.0/Human2 credit is resolved as described above.

The still-open decisions are the exact historical CCLE permissions for the intended future distribution, participant-linked derived-output disposition, secondary-use ethics determination, ownership/licensing of the new package, and the human author/funding/conflict declarations. No public submission, deposit, rehosting or rights-holder contact is authorized. See the [author checklist](author-checklist.md). The current technical work and an internal aggregate review package do not manufacture those declarations.

## Notice retention for a later authorized release

Retain the old Apache-2.0 and CC BY 4.0 notices with their scoped material; preserve upstream dataset/model attribution and exact versions; mark modifications and new output provenance; identify any files deliberately omitted for rights or privacy reasons; and record the rightsholder-approved terms for new code/content. Do not copy a single licence label across all components. A later release decision must review actual package contents against this register.
